CHAPTER SPINE
────────────

  Preface — Who This Book Is For and What It Will Teach You

  Chapter  1 — The Claim
               Code is dog food for the JIT compiler. I stopped treating
               source code as literature and handed the read/write lock
               exclusively to the LLM.

             — The Open Field
               Programming has always been an open field. Vibe coding as
               democratisation, the BASIC magazine era, the gatekeeping
               impulse, and why beginners have the advantage.

  Chapter  2 — The Verification Surface
               Invariants replace review. How to build sensors that catch
               entire categories of failure, and why line-by-line review
               is a substitute for meaningful guarantees.

  Chapter  3 — Directed AI Analysis
               You don't read code. You read the AI's reasoning about the
               code. You argue with it, you push back, you reject wrong
               explanations until it converges on the right one.

  Chapter  4 — Technical Credit
               The new economic unit of software development. Traditional
               technical debt accrues interest. Directed AI development
               accrues interest-bearing credit.

  Chapter  5 — The Adversarial Loop
               Debugging through dialogue, not through code reading.
               Decades of experience calibrate your bullshit detector.
               You test hypotheses through conversation, not inspection.

  Chapter  6 — The Sensor Network
               Each bug fix adds a permanent invariant. Each invariant
               prevents that entire class of failure forever. The
               verification surface compounds with every iteration.

  Chapter  7 — Code as Derived Artifact
               The canonical hierarchy is inverted. Invariants and
               observed behavior are the source of truth. Code is a
               transient, regeneratable output — like MySQL's ibdata1.

  Chapter  8 — The No-Peeking Rule
               Why you must never look at the code, and what happens
               when you do. The discipline required to maintain the
               paradigm, and how it forces better verification.

  Chapter  9 — The Case Study
               YehThatRocks by the numbers: 250K LOC, $300, three months.
               Architecture, PRs, invariants, production incidents, and
               what actually went wrong (and didn't).

  Chapter 10 — Paradigm Shift
               What this means for the industry. Why the LinkedIn skeptics
               are doing the math from the wrong balance sheet. How the
               role of "developer" changes when code is no longer the
               artifact of record.

  Appendix A — The Conversation
               The full CodeWhale session transcript that articulated the
               paradigm in real time — including the part where the AI
               got it wrong and the author pushed back.

  Appendix B — The Verification Scripts
               A catalog of the invariant scripts that guard YehThatRocks,
               annotated with what each guards against and how it was
               commissioned.

  Appendix C — The Prompting Patterns
               The specific patterns of direction, argument, and
               commissioning that produce working code from an LLM
               without reading the output.

  Appendix D — The Numbers
               Tokens consumed, costs per feature, PR acceptance rates,
               production incidents, and the economics of the approach.

  Appendix E — Your First AI-Assisted Project
               A concrete getting-started guide for beginners. Version
               control, describing features, manual QA, arguing with
               the AI, and adding your first invariant. With a BASIC
               GOTO note for advanced readers.


MANUSCRIPT NOTE
───────────────
Chapters are written as .txt files in this folder. Each chapter is a
complete, self-contained piece of prose suitable for conversion to any
publishing format. The tone is direct, unapologetic, and grounded in the
specifics of a project that actually shipped. No hand-waving. No hype.
Just the approach, the evidence, and the argument.
