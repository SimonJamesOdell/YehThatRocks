This appendix provides the quantitative data behind the YehThatRocks
case study. All numbers are approximate but grounded in the actual
development process from April to July 2026.

  Provider pricing: approximately $0.43 per million input tokens
  No other AI service costs (self-hosted where possible)

  VPS hosting: ~$12/month (Hetzner)
  Domain: standard registration
  YouTube Data API: free tier (10,000 units/day)
  No other paid services

Development tools:
  CodeWhale: free tier sufficient
  VSCode: free
  GitHub: free
  No paid IDE plugins or AI subscriptions beyond API usage

Total project cost: approximately $350 including hosting

Incidents

Production incidents reaching users: fewer than 10
  Duplicate migration failures: ~4 (before validation system)
  Connection pool exhaustion: 2

  Mobile rendering issues: 2
  No security incidents reported

Pre-production failures caught by invariants: unknown (hundreds)

  Average: ~30 minutes (from symptom observation to fix verified)
  Fastest: ~15 minutes (mobile duplicate artist names)

Comparison to Traditional Development

If YehThatRocks had been built traditionally:

A project of this scope — a full-stack web application with
authentication, video integration, real-time features, an admin
dashboard, a magazine, a forum, and mobile support — would typically
require a team of three to five developers and would take considerably
longer than three months. A realistic estimate might be nine to
eighteen months for a team of that size, depending on experience and
whether they were working full-time.

At UK market rates, a senior developer costs approximately £80,000 per
year. A team of three seniors for twelve months would cost roughly
£240,000 in salary alone — before infrastructure, tooling, or
management overhead. At US rates, the figure is higher still.

The AI-assisted approach shipped the same scope in three months with
one developer directing. The AI API cost was approximately $300. But
that is not the relevant cost comparison, because the developer was
still working for those three months — directing, arguing, verifying,
and designing. The developer's time is by far the most expensive
component.

What the AI replaced was not the developer. It was the other two to
four developers on the hypothetical team, and most of the calendar
time. One person, amplified by an LLM and guarded by a verification
surface, produced the output of a small team in a fraction of the
time.

The economic win is not that the AI API bill is cheap. The economic
win is that one experienced developer, working with an AI, can ship
what would otherwise require a team — and can do it faster. The API
bill is nearly noise compared to the value of the time saved and
the team not hired.

In numbers:

  Traditional approach (estimated):
    Team: 3-5 senior developers
    Timeline: 9-18 months
    Salary cost: £240,000-£360,000 (UK) or $540,000-$810,000 (US)
    Infrastructure and tooling: additional
    Management overhead: additional
    Risk: hiring, onboarding, team churn, coordination tax

  AI-assisted approach (actual):
    Team: 1 developer (director)
    Timeline: 3 months
    Developer time: 3 months of full-time directing
    AI API cost: ~$300
    Infrastructure: ~$36 (3 months hosting)
    Total out-of-pocket: ~$350

The cost difference is not the $300 API bill versus a developer's
salary. It is one person's attention versus a team's attention, and
three months versus a year or more. The AI did not make the developer
cheaper. It made the developer cover more ground.

These comparisons are imperfect. The AI-assisted approach is not a
drop-in replacement for a traditional team. It requires a specific
set of skills and a specific discipline. Not every developer can
(or wants to) work this way. But for those who can, the economics
are transformative — not because the AI is cheap, but because the
developer's time is now leveraged across a much larger surface area.


Token Usage (Approximate)

These numbers are estimates based on the CodeWhale session logs and
API usage patterns:

Average session length: ~50,000-100,000 tokens
Sessions per feature: 2-5 (commission, debug, refine, invariant)
Total sessions over 3 months: ~200-300
Total tokens: approximately 15-30 million

At $0.43/million input tokens and higher output token costs, the
total API spend of ~$300 is consistent with these estimates.

The cost per feature is dominated by the adversarial loop — arguing
with the AI until it converges on the correct implementation. Features
that require less argument are cheaper. Features that require more
argument are more expensive. The verification surface reduces argument
over time, making features cheaper as the project matures.


Caveats

These numbers are from one project by one developer. They are not
statistically significant. They may not generalize. Your project may
require more or less argument, more or fewer invariants, more or less
cost.

The point of this appendix is not to promise specific results. It is
to demonstrate that the directed AI paradigm is economically viable
for at least one class of project (complex web application) by at
least one developer (experienced, disciplined, adversarial).

The paradigm is young. The tools are improving. The economics will
shift. But the direction of the shift is clear: the cost of software
is trending toward the cost of directing it, not the cost of writing
it. This appendix is a snapshot of that trend at an early point on
the curve.
